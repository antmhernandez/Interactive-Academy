What does a motion graph reveal?

Name Date
Question: How can measured position and time reveal velocity and acceleration?

Objective: Compare linear and quadratic models, read their coefficients, and examine whether the residuals support the chosen model.

Materials: Recorded motion data or the sample observations available in the Motion Data Laboratory.

1. Predict before calculating

Sketch the position graph you expect for constant velocity. How would it differ for constant acceleration?

2. Record observations

Choose an origin and a positive direction. If you record your own data, include actual measurement units and explain how time was measured.

3. Fit two models

Open the Motion Data Laboratory and enter the observations. First fit the linear model, then the quadratic model.

Linear model: x(t) = A + Bt

A = m   B = m/s

Quadratic model: x(t) = A + Bt + Ct²

A = m   B = m/s   C = m/s²

Estimated acceleration from quadratic model, a = 2C = m/s².

4. Examine what the fitted line misses

For each model, compare the data points, fitted curve, residual plot, and RMS residual. Which appears more appropriate, and why? Do not rely on R² alone.

Does a curved position graph necessarily prove the acceleration was constant? Explain.

5. Explain the physics

  1. What does a negative fitted B value mean when you have chosen right as positive?
  2. Why is the acceleration 2C rather than C?
  3. What physical assumption makes a quadratic model appropriate, and what experimental effects could make it imperfect?
